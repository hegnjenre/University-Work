More on Cloud Computing

‘Amazon launched the first commercial cloud – the Elastic Computer Cloud (EC2) in 2006’, stated the cybermagazine in 2021. This changed the way we manage, store and process data making it easier for many users to access data via the internet when they may need it. With a market size of now over a hundred billion dollars Cloud Computing has significantly grown and dominated many aspects of the tech industry within the past few years. However, due to its massive growth in turn Cloud Computing takes up a substantial amount of energy and power to store everyone’s data. In the ‘Cloud Computing: Survey on Energy Efficiency’ they state that “1.1% and 1.5% of the total electricity use worldwide” is taken from large cloud data centres. This is turn proves that as much as Cloud Computing is an innovative idea it can also have many negative aspects.

Furthering the point on Cloud computing taking up significant amounts of energy, which has now become a concern with global climate change. Therefore, green cloud computing is being investigated to reduce the environmental impact. To tackle this matter and implement elements of green cloud computing companies have to take multiple strategies which can include; Energy sources(using renewable energy to store data such as solar and wind power), facilities(for example locating a data centre in a cold or hot climate), infrastructure(utilizing resources to the best of their abilities to reduce the number of servers and storage devices)and workflow(finding ways to optimise IT workflows at every level). Many companies are switching to green cloud practises and companies such as Amazon and Microsoft plan to used 100% renewable energy in their data centres by 2025.

In a recent report, from SpaceLift more than 60% of corporate data is stored on the cloud, personal data has more than doubled from 1.1 billion in 2024 and on average workers use 36 cloud-based services per day. A solution to decreasing the amount of dark data we have stored on the cloud is that we must regularly go through and delete old data, by removing this inessential data a small amount of carbon can be saved.

Other effects as a result of data being stored can furthermore lead to problems such as data leakage, data loss and account or service hijacking. To prevent any of the unknown user’s location and information from being breached Cloud Computing companies must ensure that confidentiality of data is not compromised. Therefore, the security mechanisms must be regularly updated and inspected as technology continues to develop and evolve. As a result, when cloud services are hacked criminals are given access to company’s dark data, and consequently more financial costs are spent on cybersecurity in companies to protect being involved in a data breach on data. Additionally, Clouds can also vary dependant on what brand you are using which means you may have limited control on key administrative tasks such as updates, this can irritate users who like to be in control of their storage as an update can take up a lot of storage and can occasionally cause issues.

Although a benefit of Cloud Computing storage is that it’s never a concern of running out, Cloud Computing can hold so much storage enforcing us as users to not have any concern about saving useless data and never looking at it again. Unfortunately, this means in turn we can build up old data on our cloud from years ago which will never be binned, this is dark data. The Economist states that between 70% and 90% of data captured by an organisation is dark data, which not only effects the environment but also can finically hurt a company to store in their systems. The only way to decrease this is to make sure data is used sustainably to maximise their business value.
