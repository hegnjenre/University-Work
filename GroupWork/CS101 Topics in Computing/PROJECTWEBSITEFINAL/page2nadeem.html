Data warehouses also have an improved data quality as they use a process called data cleansing, which is the action of assessing and fixing defective, wrong or frankly useless data. According to one article the most frequent problems in data are “missing values, misplaced entries, and typographical errors.” (Talend, 2019) This could seriously affect your businesses data, especially in terms of value, and the proof is clearly visible, in America alone, organizations lose over $3 trillion a year due to these frequent issues, so data cleansing is an extremely important step!

More recently, data warehouses are being used for artificial intelligence, this is because data warehouses are also extremely good at analysing historical data, such as recognising frequent or unique patterns, teaching these sorts of results to an Artificial Intelligence application would be extremely beneficial to spotting reoccurring losses.

Creating photos is a very common occurrence nowadays, with the improvement of camera quality on mobile phones, and the simplicity that comes with pressing a single button to capture whatever is in front of you has definitely increased the number of photos that are taken. According to a recent article, throughout the entire year of 2024 an astonishing 1.94 trillion photos will have been taken and that 94% of them will come from smartphones! (Broz, 2024)

  • I created some of my own statistics with my group to see roughly how much data we are creating and how much storage we take up on a yearly basis, I asked them to make a note of how many photos they took in one day, out of our five, we had a total of 16, I then asked them each for the size in Megabytes of one of their photos, allowing me to create an average of 4.72MB. After just a year, we created 27,489.28MB of photos between just five people!

  • The most recent census of the United Kingdom’s population is 67.6 million people (Team, 2024), now only 6% of the population of the UK in 2020 had no access whatsoever to the internet (Prescott, 2021), so with our own statistics we can estimate just how much data is being created using photos yearly by the residents of the United Kingdom.

    • In photos alone, the UK creates 333,175 terabytes of data a year in estimation! Now the majority of photos on smartphones are automatically uploaded to some sort of cloud database whether it is iCloud or Google Drive, which creates a huge incentive to build more and more data warehouses, in turn using more and more electricity to fulfil the growing need for more storage. According to one article “the Cloud now has a greater carbon footprint than the airline industry. A single data centre can consume the equivalent electricity of 50,000 homes. At 200 terawatt hours (TWh) annually, data centres collectively devour more energy than some nation-states”. (Salahuddin & Gorman, 2023)

    This is clearly having a devasting effect on the environment, but is often missed and ignored by the public, if more light was shed on this topic maybe the effects could be reduced, and we could find more environmentally friendly ways to enjoy our data.
