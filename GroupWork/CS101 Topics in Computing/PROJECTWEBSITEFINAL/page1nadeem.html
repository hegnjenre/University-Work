Data warehousing is a very useful tool in our day in age, large businesses use it to handle and run extremely strong analytics on vast amounts of data. The more basic type of database wouldn’t be able to handle such a vast amount of information, building data warehouses is invaluable to any sort of major system. For example, they are commonly used in point of sales (POS), such as a till in a supermarket, they are used in our day-to-day life, and we don’t even notice it! (Holdsworth & Kosinski, 2024)

Most data warehouses are set up on a three-level plan for processing data in a skilful and swift manner, starting from the bottom level. This is where the data comes from multiple different roots which then filter into a warehouse, according to IBM’s topic of data warehouses, “The data is most often moved through a process known as extract, transform, load (ETL) or sometimes a process known as extract, load, transform (ELT).” (Holdsworth & Kosinski, 2024). Both methods are efficient but do it in different ways.

The next level is extremely important, most data warehouses are structured around an online analytical processing system, or some sort of analytics engine. The reason this is so vital, it allows the analysis of multiple databases from many different sources at an unprecedented speed, which is extremely helpful when quick analytics are needed.

The top level is a more hands on and visual tool, which allows the user to check their own results on their data which can lead to identifying issues or problems within their business, in turn allowing them to alter their choices to benefit them without missing any information. Some also have a sandbox, which is a testing ground of sort, “a copy of the production data and relevant analysis and visualization tools. Data analysts and data scientists can experiment with new analytical techniques in the sandbox without impacting the operations of the data warehouse for other users.” (Holdsworth & Kosinski, 2024)
