To tackle exactly how much dark data my group use we each looked at three key types of electronic data we use on the daily: emojis, emails, photos. In the final part of this essay, I go into detail of my findings and how much I use these data types and how much they impact my cloud storage and what I can do to resolve this going forward.

Firstly, I recorded my emoji use over the past 7 days,and I averaged at around 21 emojis sent per day. This means that I roughly 147 emojis sent per week. An emoji character consumes roughly 4 bytes but can be more depending on if it consists of 2 code points. Over 6 billion emojis are sent on a daily basis and out of phone users over 80% regularly use emojis. People could reduce their emoji usage or delete useless emojis they do not need on their keyboards.

Secondly, I recorded my email use over the past 7 days, and I averaged at about 17 emails received to my account per day. This means that I roughly averaged around 119 emails sent per week. According to TechTarget a short email can roughly consume around 5KB. Over 8.32 billion emails are sent daily by the UK and 85% of the UK’s population use emails according to the UK Fundraising. People can help reduce the number of emails they store by binning old emails therefore they won’t take up unnecessary storage.

Thirdly, I recorded my photo use over the past 7 days, and I averaged at about taking 5 per day. This means I roughly averaged around 35 photos taken per week. According to TechTarget a low-resolution photograph is around 100 KB. People can reduce the number of photos they store by deleting pictures regularly that they do not need anymore. As much as cloud computing is great for accessing images anywhere although it is noted it can be slow to load and access if there is any sort of internet disruption.

In conclusion, throughout my findings I have gathered that cloud computing is very useful tool and a great piece of modern technology if used wisely. However, it can still be a concern especially with the amount of energy usage and its environmental impact. As a result, we can see that we use Cloud Computing daily and that it does in fact gather more dark data than we realise.
