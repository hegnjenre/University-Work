Reducing Organisations digital Footprint

Organisations need to quickly recognise the harm of dark data and reduce their digital carbon footprint by finding and using the data already collected as according to the International Data Corporation (IDC) found every week information workers in western Europe lose 50% of their time searching and 20% doing tasks that have already been done, this adds up to be 10 hours a week collecting data already collected before and organisation do not realise how much carbon emissions they are creating as according to the OPSI “a typical data-driven enterprise with 50 full-time employees will generate 2,295 GB of data a day, this equates to 550,800 GB of data generated a year, which will produce 1,102 tons of C02 per year” This therefore uses up more space and more energy that the organisation did not need to use which continues to add to the carbon footprint. Organisations need to analyse their data and optimise use out of their data to reduce time spent and waste to minimise their impact on the environment. By using dark data, organisations can benefit from a deeper insight into customer behaviour and make more informed decisions to help them grow. . Many organisations such as IBM have found ways to reduce the environmental implications of dark data, this is due to the creation of “IBM Watsonx.data” which “allows you to maximise value from existing data investments” and “reducing duplication”.

In conclusion, dark data contributes to a plethora of the digital carbon emissions such as resource waste: including the vast amount of energy required for data centres to house servers and storage systems. As well as the inefficient resource allocation – resulting in an opportunity cost as organisations could invest into greener technologies, lowering costs for the firms in the long run due to lower storage costs, reduced energy expenses and less resources being used, as well as reducing greenhouse gas emissions. However, if organisations were to manage dark data – it can potentially have a positive impact on their planning and overall data quality by maintaining only relevant and accurate information, it can result in a beneficial outcome.
